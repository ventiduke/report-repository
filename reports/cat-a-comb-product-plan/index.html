Cat-a-Comb product plan: prototype in 4 weeks, trial with 8–10 cats, decide in week 9

The pitch, the spec, how to build it, how to keep it safe, what it might cost, how we'd test it with real cats, and how we'd launch it, all for about $0.7k–1.4k in materials before any production commitment.

By A. Montecalvo Published Oct 7, 2026 Companion to the design brief

The short version

  1. The pitch: a cat bed that's also a tunnel and looks like furniture. Our own cats chose the shape before we designed it: soft walls on both sides, a curved floor, cover overhead.
  2. The product: a flat-pack wood frame with a flexible foam sling hung between two removable rails, in a zip-off washable cover. The standard size is about 26″ × 11½″ × 11″ inside, in an open or a covered version.
  3. The risk is demand and price, not engineering. At small-batch cost, roughly $150–190 per unit (planning estimate), a sustainable price is about $300+. That is premium territory, so we test the price before we build stock.
  4. The plan: week 1, a cardboard size check; weeks 2–4, two prototypes; weeks 5–8, a home trial with 8–10 cats plus a waitlist price test; week 9, go or no-go on a first batch of 25–50 units.

01The pitch: furniture your cat already chose

Cats look for snug, enclosed spots with walls to lean on, and owners improvise them out of boxes, blankets and the space under the coffee table. What's usually on offer is plush or plastic "pet gear" that looks out of place in a living room.

  • Customer: design-conscious cat owners, especially in smaller homes where every piece is on show; multi-cat households; and gift buyers.
  • Promise: a bed and a tunnel in one, built like furniture, easy to clean, and proven by real cats. We lead with photos and video of cats actually using it.
  • Why it could win:
    • The shape comes from observed behaviour, not a guess.
    • The sling construction is simple, flat-packs, and can be repaired.
    • Covers, foam and scratch pads are replaceable, which creates repeat accessory sales.
  • What it must not claim: health or anxiety benefits. We have no evidence for those, and pet-product claims attract scrutiny.

In the box: two end frames, two base rails, two sling rails, the foam sling in its cover, four non-slip feet, bolts and a hex key, plus a roof panel for the Covered model. Accessories: spare covers (colours, faux-fur topper), a replacement foam sheet, an end-mounted cardboard or sisal scratch pad, and later a connector kit so units join into a longer tunnel.

Why these sizes: the concept boards' 24″ × 10″ × 10″ is an outside size, which leaves only ~8½″ of width inside the frame. That's tight for an average cat lying on its side. 11½″ inside gives room to stretch and turn; the Large size covers big breeds. The week-1 cardboard mock-up checks this with our own cats before any wood is cut.

03The build: a knock-down frame and a foam sling, no moulds

The frame is two end frames joined by bolted base rails. Two round rails rest in notches cut into the tops of the end frames and pass through sleeves in the cover. The foam hangs between them like a hammock and forms the U under its own weight and the cat's, just like the folded mat our cats chose.

Prototype build steps

  1. Mock-up first (week 1). Tape a cardboard box to the inside size, hang a foam offcut between two broom handles, and see whether our cats use it. Adjust width and depth before buying hardwood.
  2. Mill and cut the posts and rails to the cut list. Sand all edges to a soft radius; cats rub against corners.
  3. Make the end frames: dowel-joint the posts and end rails, glue, clamp and check square.
  4. Cut the sling-rail notches (1″ round-bottom) at the top inside corners, deep enough that rails can't roll out under load.
  5. Drill for knock-down fittings: cross-dowel holes in the base rails, bolt holes in the end frames.
  6. Finish: a non-toxic, water-based finish such as a toy-safe (EN 71-3) product. Cure fully before any cat use; no oily or solvent finishes.
  7. Make the sling: cut the foam; sew the cover with two rail sleeves and a hidden underside zip (a local upholsterer can do this if we don't sew).
  8. Assemble: bolt the base rails between the end frames, slide the rails through the sleeves, and drop them into the notches. Add the feet, and the roof for the Covered model.
  9. Load test: 15 lb (6.8 kg) sandbag dropped into the sling 50 times; check the notches, sleeves and seams. Rock the frame to check it doesn't tip or slide.
  10. Time the customer assembly from flat-pack. Target: under 10 minutes with the included hex key.

Tools: mitre or table saw, drill with a 1″ Forstner bit, dowel jig, clamps, sander, sewing machine (or an upholsterer).

What that implies for price

  • Selling direct at roughly 2× unit cost (about 50% gross margin before shipping and marketing) implies about $300–375 at small-batch cost. That's premium for a cat bed, so the waitlist price test (section 06) has to show demand at that level.
  • Selling through shops usually needs a retail price around 4× cost, which isn't viable until cost falls.
  • Levers to bring cost down: CNC-cut or pre-cut kits (labour toward ~¾ h), birch or oak instead of walnut, simpler covers, and batches of 50+. At ~¾ h of labour, a walnut unit falls to about $140 (estimate).

06Testing: real cats, a real price signal, and criteria agreed up front

Phase 0 · Size mock-up · week 1 · ~$20–50

A cardboard and foam mock-up at the recommended inside size, tried with our two cats. It tells us width and sling depth before any wood is cut.

Phase 1 · Bench prototypes · weeks 2–4 · ~$200–400 materials

One Standard Open and one Standard Covered (one with acrylic sides, one without), in walnut. Load, tip and assembly tests from section 03; safety checklist from section 04.

Phase 2 · Home trial + price test · weeks 5–8 · ~$450–900 materials

Trial: 8–10 birch units placed with colleagues' cats for 4 weeks. Include at least 2 large cats, 2 multi-cat homes and 2 shy cats. Owners keep a 1-line daily diary (used? roughly how long?) and do short surveys at weeks 2 and 4, with photos where they're happy to share.

Price test: a simple waitlist page with real cat photos, showing different visitors one of 2–3 price points; we measure sign-ups at each price.

Supply: quotes from 2–3 makers for frames, covers and foam; a trademark search on the name.

07Launch: sell a small batch direct, then add accessories

  1. Waitlist first. Real-cat photos and short videos are the marketing. Cats in boxes are endlessly shareable, and trial owners' content (with permission) is the proof.
  2. First batch of 25–50, sold direct through our own site, or a handmade marketplace such as Etsy if that's faster to set up. Pre-orders where possible, so cash comes in before production.
  3. Accessories from day one: spare covers, a faux-fur topper and a scratch pad. These are repeat purchases that lift order value.
  4. Then grow the range: the Large size, the connector kit for longer tunnels, and the bookshelf module, once the core is proven and cost is low enough for shop margins.
  5. Policies: flat-pack shipping; returns accepted only if unused (hygiene); a clear care guide.
